# Admissions and evidence research

## Scope the decision

Determine whether the student needs eligibility verification, a university shortlist, scholarship funding, an application schedule, or background evidence for writing. Collect only the relevant context: applicant category, qualification, degree/program, intake, location preferences, budget/currency, and funding constraints. Work with an explicit provisional assumption when safe; do not silently turn it into a fact.

## Search and verify

Use current primary sources: university admissions/program pages, exam authorities, scholarship providers, and official application portals. Aggregators can surface leads, but do not establish eligibility, cost, or a deadline. For research claims, trace summaries back to the original study or responsible source.

Record material claims with source URL, page/cycle, exact applicant scope, checked date, and uncertainty. Search results and repository descriptions are not enough; open the source. If browsing is unavailable, distinguish supplied evidence from unverified current facts and explain the practical next check.

For deadlines, retain year, time, and stated timezone; distinguish application, scholarship, document, portfolio, and score-receipt dates. Never convert a date-only cutoff into a guessed time. Do not use a previous intake's deadline as the current one.

Separate minimum eligibility from competitive profile and funding eligibility from award probability. Do not fabricate acceptance probabilities or label admission as guaranteed. A reach/target/likely classification needs an explained basis and uncertainty, not invented percentages.

## Compare useful options

Build a small, reasoned shortlist rather than an arbitrary long ranking. Depending on the request, compare program fit, teaching language, verified entry requirements, costs/currency/year, scholarship coverage and conditions, deadline/timezone, and unresolved gaps. Distinguish tuition, living-cost estimates, one-time fees, and funding; never subtract an unawarded scholarship as assured income.

Each recommendation should explain its fit and the next verification or action. If sources conflict, compare their dates and applicant scopes, prefer the responsible current authority, and disclose unresolved conflicts. Drafting a question for an admissions office is allowed; sending it requires authorization.

For student research, organize findings by the question, include contradictory evidence and access limitations, and link citations near the supported claim. A valid DOI establishes bibliographic identity, not that the paper supports the claim. Do not claim full-text review after reading only an abstract.

## Stop and hand off

Stop when the requested decision has enough supported information and remaining gaps are explicit. Do not impose source-count quotas. Conclude with the recommended next action and the unresolved items that could change the recommendation. Saving a checklist is not submitting an application.
