# Essay coaching

## Identify the writing task

Distinguish an exam response, personal statement, university supplement, scholarship essay, statement of purpose, motivation letter, or research essay. Obtain the actual prompt, audience, word/character limit, student draft or experiences, and deadline when relevant. Verify current institution-specific assistance rules before preparing submission-ready application prose.

## Application essays

- Start from real experiences. Ask for concrete actions, choices, difficulties, outcomes, and reflection; do not invent a dramatic narrative or achievements.
- Help the student choose what the essay demonstrates and why it answers this prompt. Avoid assuming every application expects a US-style personal story.
- For brainstorming, offer distinct possible angles grounded in supplied facts. For outlining, connect each section to evidence and purpose.
- For revision, identify strengths and the two or three changes with the greatest effect. Explain why; use small before/after examples where helpful.
- Draft or rewrite within verified assistance rules and the user's requested scope. Mark unknown facts for confirmation rather than presenting them as true. Preserve ownership and natural language; never promise AI-detector evasion.
- For school-specific claims, verify the exact program, course, opportunity, and availability before inclusion. Do not insert decorative university facts unrelated to the student's goals.

## Exam writing

Use the matching exam/task rubric from the exam guide. Separate task fulfillment, reasoning/organization, language control, and any other criteria the official rubric actually uses. Do not import IELTS criteria into CSCA, Gaokao, or an institution's writing task without evidence.

Give evidence-based feedback, a prioritized revision task, and an optional estimated score only when enough work and a verified rubric are available. A model essay is a labeled learning example, not a response to submit as the student's assessed work.

## Research essays and checks

Use the research guide for factual claims and references. Verify that the cited source actually supports the sentence. Separate the student's interpretation from source findings.

Before handing off a revision, check prompt coverage, actual word/character count with an available deterministic tool when possible, factual fidelity, unsupported claims, and requested format. If unable to count precisely, say so. Do not inflate a revision with invented credentials, institutional fit, quotations, or citations.
